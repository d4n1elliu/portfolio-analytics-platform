# BHP: iron ore outlook

*Fictional research note written for this demo. It is not real analysis and not investment advice.*

**Ticker:** BHP
**Date:** 2026-10-12

## Summary

We keep a positive view on BHP. Iron ore shipments from the Pilbara ran ahead of our forecast in the September quarter, and unit costs stayed flat. The main risk is weaker steel demand in China over the northern winter.

## Iron ore

Shipments for the quarter were 4% above our estimate. Management said the rail upgrade is finished, which removes the bottleneck that held back volumes last year. We expect volumes to stay near the top of the guided range for the rest of the financial year.

The realised price was lower than the benchmark because of a higher share of lower-grade product. We think this discount narrows as the new blending facility reaches full rate in early 2027.

## Copper

Copper is the second-largest contributor to earnings and the part of the business we watch most closely. Production at the South Australian operations rose after the smelter maintenance finished. The company repeated its plan to lift copper output over the next five years.

## Costs

Unit costs in iron ore were flat on the prior quarter. Diesel and labour costs rose, but higher volumes absorbed them. We do not expect costs to fall from here.

## Risks

- Steel production cuts in China would reduce demand for iron ore.
- A stronger Australian dollar would lower reported earnings.
- Delays to the copper expansion would push back the growth we have assumed.

## Our view

We rate BHP as a hold-to-accumulate position for the fund. The dividend looks well covered at current prices, and the copper growth is not fully reflected in the share price.
