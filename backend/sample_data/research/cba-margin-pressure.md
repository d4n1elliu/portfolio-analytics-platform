# CBA: margin pressure and a full valuation

*Fictional research note written for this demo. It is not real analysis and not investment advice.*

**Ticker:** CBA
**Date:** 2026-09-03

## Summary

Commonwealth Bank reported a solid result, but its net interest margin fell and the shares trade at a large premium to the other major banks. We would not add to the position at this price.

## Net interest margin

The margin fell by four basis points over the half. Competition for home loans was the main cause: the bank matched lower rates from rivals to keep its share of new lending. Deposit pricing also moved against it as customers shifted savings into higher-rate accounts.

Management expects the margin to be stable from here. We are more cautious, because two smaller lenders have announced new mortgage offers since the result.

## Credit quality

Arrears rose slightly but remain low by historical standards. The bank holds more provisions than its models require, which gives it room if unemployment rises.

## Capital and dividends

The capital ratio is well above the regulator's minimum. The bank raised its final dividend and extended its share buy-back. We see the dividend as safe.

## Valuation

CBA trades on a higher multiple of earnings than any other large Australian bank. The quality of the franchise explains part of that premium, but not all of it. A small disappointment in earnings could move the share price a long way.

## Our view

We rate CBA as a hold. The business is strong, the price is high, and we would trim rather than add on any further rise.
