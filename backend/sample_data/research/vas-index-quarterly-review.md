# VAS: quarterly review of the Australian index fund

*Fictional research note written for this demo. It is not real analysis and not investment advice.*

**Ticker:** VAS
**Date:** 2026-10-02

## Summary

The Vanguard Australian Shares Index ETF follows the 300 largest companies listed in Australia. It is the fund's benchmark and also its largest single holding. This note reviews what drove the index over the September quarter.

## What drove the index

Banks and miners make up more than half of the index, so it moves mostly with those two groups. Over the quarter the banks rose and the miners were flat, which left the index slightly ahead.

Health care was the strongest of the smaller sectors. Consumer staples was the weakest, as supermarkets lowered prices.

## Concentration

The ten largest companies are close to half of the index. Anyone holding VAS alongside large positions in the same banks and miners is more concentrated than the number of holdings suggests. The fund should check its combined exposure to the largest banks.

## Costs and tracking

The management fee is among the lowest of any Australian share fund. The fund tracked its index closely over the quarter, with the small difference explained by the fee.

## Use in the portfolio

VAS gives broad exposure at low cost and is a sensible place to hold money that is waiting for a specific idea. It is also the benchmark the fund's own return is compared with, so holding it can never add or subtract from relative performance.

## Our view

We rate VAS as a core holding. No change is recommended.
